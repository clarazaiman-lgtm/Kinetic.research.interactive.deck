The insurgent fiber provider, mid-relaunch.

Kinetic is Uniti's residential fiber internet brand, running its own network across 18 states in secondary and rural U.S. markets. In late 2025 it named McCann Detroit as Agency of Record and relaunched under a new platform — "Internet Better" — after years of quiet, utility-style marketing.

Why this matters right now

The timing lines up

This research and the new Master Creative Brief land almost exactly on top of Kinetic's actual rebrand. The brief is effectively asking to formalize and systematize what McCann's "Internet Better" launch already started — not build something from scratch.

The tagline has real equity

"Internet better" isn't fresh copy. A trademark filing dates it to roughly 2019, and it was already in use at physical Kinetic Connection Centers years before McCann's involvement. The brief can lean on that history rather than treating it as fragile new language.

Company Overview

What Kinetic actually does.

Kinetic runs physical fiber-optic cable to homes — mostly in smaller cities, suburbs, and rural areas — and sells the internet, streaming-TV bundle, and phone service that runs over it. In plain terms: the local phone company that replaced old copper lines with fiber, trying hard not to feel like an old phone company.

Kinetic

Insurgent / Local Value

Challenger fiber provider for smaller markets; simplicity and no-nonsense pricing as the core pitch. Differentiator: network density in secondary/tertiary markets where it's often the only real fiber alternative.

The real competitive read

Kinetic isn't trying to out-scale Xfinity, AT&T, or Verizon — it can't, and doesn't. In most of its actual markets the real "competitor" is aging DSL or satellite, not another gigabit fiber brand. Overlap risk is lowest with Spectrum/Xfinity/Fios (different geography, different infrastructure) and highest with T-Mobile Home Internet, which is deliberately expanding fiber into the same underserved geography Kinetic depends on.

McCann Detroit + directors The Guard Brothers (Audi, Nike, GM). An anthemic hero spot plus comedy vignette "Problems" — unnamed cable reps hiding in a closet, "hidden fees" camouflaged in the yard. No confirmed recurring character yet.

What the rest of the category is running (2025–2026)

"Imagine That" — Goodby Silverstein & Partners

A two-minute short film reimagining Frankenstein's monster "Frank," produced with Universal Pictures and Universal Parks using the actual Monsters Unchained ride set. Extended into a Super Bowl LX spot reimagining Jurassic Park with the original cast — Sam Neill, Laura Dern, Jeff Goldblum. Major national platform, Hollywood-tier production and IP.

"America's Connectivity Company" — VCCP

A Super Bowl LX spot honoring hardworking Americans and Spectrum's "90,000 U.S.-based employees," emphasizing an all-domestic workforce and fiber network. Ran across nearly 60 markets. Separately runs "Designed for Business; Built for You" for its business/enterprise lines.

Reliability-guarantee messaging, feat. Luke Wilson

A campaign directly rebutting T-Mobile's network claims, anchored by the "AT&T Guarantee" covering wireless and fiber outages. Separately, AT&T just ran its first-ever nationwide ad bundling wireless and home internet under one message during an NFL playoff broadcast — a deliberate strategic shift confirmed by CEO John Stankey on an earnings call.

No single unifying platform found

High volume of comedic celebrity spots (Mike Myers/Seth Green "Dr. Evil," World Cup creative with David Beckham), but these skew mobile/wireless-plan focused. No equivalent "big idea" found for Fios specifically — arguably a white space Kinetic could claim in overlapping markets.

What this means for Kinetic

Scale gap is real and quantifiable

Xfinity and Spectrum are both running Super Bowl-tier national campaigns with Hollywood-level production value. Kinetic's platform is a genuine national multichannel push, but nowhere near that budget tier — no Super Bowl spot, no licensed IP, no A-list cast.

Tone is closer to Spectrum than Xfinity

Spectrum leans into real-people, down-to-earth reliability — closer to Kinetic's "local provider you can depend on." Xfinity has gone full spectacle/entertainment-brand, a register Kinetic isn't trying to compete in.

The core creative tension

Kinetic needs to feel like the upstart taking on lazy, expensive cable incumbents — but also trustworthy enough to rely on for something as essential as home internet. "Problems" resolves this by putting cable companies in the villain seat rather than making Kinetic itself look edgy: the humor aims outward at category pain points, while the brand's own promise stays reassuring and literal.

"We're the rebels, but only against your cable bill — trust us with your Wi‑Fi."

Where Uniti Solutions sits competitively

On SD-WAN specifically, independent trackers rank Uniti (formerly Windstream) alongside Comcast Business, AT&T, Verizon, Hughes, and Lumen — smallest share of the six, but positioned with Comcast Business as the value-tier alternative to AT&T/Lumen's enterprise scale. Frost & Sullivan specifically calls it out for customer satisfaction and value focus even where it trails on raw scale — a real, defensible, if quiet, position.

The Problem & Barriers to Switching

Every barrier the brief names is measurable, not assumed:

  • Low trust — ACSI 70 vs. 66 average, but fiber-specific rank is only 4th of 4 major providers.
  • Lingering reputation — J.D. Power once placed Kinetic just 2 points above CenturyLink, Suddenlink, and HughesNet.
  • Installation anxiety / outdated equipment — echoed almost verbatim in real customer reviews.
Target Mindsets

The "we go first where others won't" rural claim has real backing: fiber-connected communities see 213% higher business growth, 10% higher self-employment, and 14–17% higher home values.

The "Streaming Newcomers" segment already has a live product hook — the 2026 YouTube TV bundle, explicitly framed by Kinetic's own SVP as "what Internet Better looks like in practice."

The Competitive Villain — why "no names" is strategy, not just legal caution

The existing "Problems" vignette already follows this rule: unnamed cable reps, not named competitors. Given Xfinity's Universal Pictures tie-in and Spectrum's 60-market Super Bowl buy, naming a specific competitor invites a scale comparison Kinetic will lose. Staying at the category level is the right call.

Tone & Style Boundaries

Kinetic's sharp/deadpan/situational lane is genuinely open ground — Xfinity has gone spectacle, Spectrum patriotic-workforce, AT&T combative-network-superiority. None overlap with Kinetic's register.

Confirmed gap: no Spanish-language creative found anywhere, despite Xfinity and Verizon both running dedicated Spanish-language spots in this category. Worth a deliberate answer before the platform locks.

Master Brand Assets

Palette and CMO/agency details check out against public rebrand coverage. The four-blade propeller logo and Figtree typeface are not public anywhere — confirm live status with brand/design before it goes into any externally-facing production spec.

Budget Reality Check

$1.6M all-in production is nowhere near Xfinity's Universal Pictures tie-in or Spectrum's Super Bowl buy. The brief's own instinct — deadpan, writing-led, non-famous-actor execution — is the correct strategic response to this budget, not a limitation. "Problems" is the proof-of-concept for what this tier can do well.
